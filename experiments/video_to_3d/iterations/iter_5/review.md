**Why 6.5 when IoU is the best yet.** The +0.004 IoU and the correct plaid and tee hues are real gains. But for a viewer, the turntable now looks blurrier and more smudged than iter_4: the plaid is doubled, the pockets are gone, the face is flat and pale, and there are orange smears at the cuffs. The texture regressed more than the colour improved.

## Step-log audit
- **Step 1 (fit): kept, correct.** It is the only source of the IoU gain, and steps 15 and 16 confirm that strength 0.7 with 2 iterations is the sweet spot (stronger or more iterations is worse at 0° and 135°).
- **Steps 2–5: good diagnosis.** step_2.png does show a patchwork, ghosted face (double eyes and mouth at 0° and 315°), and power 40 changing nothing rules out blending as the cause. The designer correctly named the root cause as frame-to-frame misregistration, but then only fenced the face off. **The same misregistration is what doubles and blurs the plaid** (power 20 at step 11 didn't help, for the same reason). The round tuned around the root cause instead of fixing it.
- **Steps 6–10: the most useful work in the round.** The head_frac/skin-test bug fix (the red tee and dark stripes had been classed as skin) is a genuine tool fix, raising coverage from 47% to 79% and correcting the tee colour. The skin_sat and skin_gamma steps are sensible, but they were judged on medians, and visually the face reads paler and flatter than the target because `unlit` removes all shading on the generated head.
- **Steps 12–13:** marginal, which the designer said honestly. **Steps 14 and 17–18:** properly rejected. The renderer flake was handled correctly.
- **The missing control is the biggest loop flaw.** No step compared "fit + skin fixes + iter_4 cloth texture, no projection" against the projected result. IoU is flat across all texture steps, so every keep/revert was a visual call made only against earlier projection steps, never against iter_4's crisp plaid. A side-by-side with iter_4 would have shown that projection costs crispness.

## What improved / regressed vs previous
- **Improved:** IoU 0.897→0.901 from the fit (biggest at 0°, 90° and 315°). Plaid hue and purple/lavender balance now match the video at every angle, including the back. The red tee is exact. The skin is no longer orange. The neck streaks are gone. The skin-classification bug in the projection tool is fixed.
- **Regressed:** plaid crispness and contrast (doubled or smeared checks, grey-ish whites, lower gradients). The pocket flaps are lost. The face is flat and featureless under unlit. There are new artefacts: tan smears at the cuffs and hem, a dark line down the placket, and a tan fringe under the beard at the side views. IoU dropped at 135° and 225°.

## Top 3 recommendations for the next round
1. **Fix the projection's root cause: per-frame registration before blending.** For each projection frame, refine its camera (yaw ±5°, dx/dy ±6 px, scale ±3%) by maximising the IoU between the rendered mesh mask and that frame's mask. `fit_silhouette` already computes per-frame calib IoU, so reuse it. Then project with winner-take-all per texel (the best-facing registered frame, `power` → ∞), feathered over about 4 texels at view boundaries, instead of a weighted blend. Step plan:
   - (a) registration only, power 8, check that the 180° checks become square and single;
   - (b) winner-take-all;
   - (c) retry `region all` for the face; it may work once frames line up.

   Success test at 6× zoom: the 180° back checks are crisp blocks like the target, the 0° pocket flaps are visible, and the torso p5/p95 is within ±8 of the target (about 32/127 at 0°).
2. **Run the missing control, then fall back to a frequency-split blend if needed.** Step A is the current recipe with `project` removed (fit + skin fixes + iter_4 texture), compared side by side with this iteration's renders at 0° and 180°. If registration (#1) doesn't make the projection at least as crisp as iter_4, add a `project.lowpass_sigma` (about 6 texels). It would take only the low-frequency colour from the projection (fixing hue and back brightness) and keep the high-frequency detail from the generated texture (keeping crisp checks and pockets). That combines iter_4's sharpness with iter_5's colour.
3. **Clean the projection's image-space inputs and bring back face shading.**
   - In each target frame, mask out skin-tone and background pixels plus a 6–8 px dilation around the hands before projecting the cloth, instead of eroding texels. That removes the cuff and hem smears and the beard fringe.
   - Mask out the dark placket line at 0°: exclude pixels within about 3 px of the tee/plaid boundary.
   - For the head, either turn `unlit` off for head texels only (use a per-material or vertex flag), or bake a soft lambert/AO term into the head texture at about 0.6 strength, so the eyes, brows and nose read again. Judge it against the target face at 0° and 315°, not against medians.
