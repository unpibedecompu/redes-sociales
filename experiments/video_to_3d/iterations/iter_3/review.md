**Overall score: 6.5/10** (best so far: 6.5/10 in iter 3; before this, iter 2 had 5.5) · mean IoU: 0.828 (green-screen harness; iter_1 0.809, iter_2 0.754)

Setup: there was no new generation. This is iter_2's raw TRELLIS mesh (8 views, multidiffusion, force_multiimage) with three post-processing steps: a 17% bottom clip, a normal-weighted gamma lift (front 1.45, back 2.0) and saturation ×0.85. Framing is correct: the front is at 0° and the back at 180°, so no `--yaw` is needed. All numbers below come from the re-scored green-screen `metrics.json`.

**The colour metric is not evidence here.** The three texture parameters were fitted against the target's mean colours, so I score texture only on what I can see. One useful check still survives: the oblique angles 45°, 90° and 135° were not part of the 2-angle calibration, and their colour diffs (15.7, 14.2, 10.7) are 2–4× the fitted angles (1.7–6.8). The true residual is therefore around 10–15, not around 4.

## What improved / regressed vs previous
- **Improved:** the clip fixed the framing. IoU rose from 0.754 to 0.828 on the same harness, and every angle is up: 0° +0.15, 180° +0.12, 225° +0.11. The front-to-back darkness falloff is gone. The back plaid now looks like the target instead of near-black, the side and back fidelity is the best of any iteration, and the hair and beard are lighter.
- **Regressed:** the skin got paler and greyer. The gamma lift is applied to all texels without distinguishing skin from cloth, so the face lost warmth and detail. The plaid got slightly softer and lower in contrast.
- **Unchanged:** the geometry. The thin side profile and the small, sloped shoulders limit 45°, 90° and 135° to 0.70–0.76, and no texture work can move that.

## Top 3 recommendations for the next iteration (most impactful first)
Quota note: recs 1 and 2 are post-processing only (`--post-only`, no Space call). Rec 3 is the only one that needs a generation; spend that single generation there.

1. **Add a body-volume post-process: non-uniform scale below the neck (quota-free).** Add `body_scale_xz: [sx, sz]` and `neck_frac` to `post` in `generate.py`. Scale only the vertices below the neck (about 22% down from the top of the head, where the mask width bottoms out at rows ~25%) around the torso's vertical axis, with a smooth ramp over about 5% of the height so the neck doesn't tear. Start with **sx 1.12, sz 1.20**, then sweep sz from 1.1 to 1.3. Measured targets: 90° and 270° torso depth needs about +20% (51 → 62 px), and 0° and 180° shoulders and chest need +10–20% (they are narrowest at the shoulder line, so an extra +5–8% at shoulder height would also help). Success test: the 90° and 270° IoU rise above 0.80 while 0° and 180° stay at 0.90 or higher. If 135° (already 10% too wide) gets worse, reduce sz before you accept the change. This is the only quota-free lever left on the weakest angles.
2. **Exclude skin from the texture lift and warm it (quota-free).** In UV space, build a skin mask: texels with hue roughly 0–40°, mid luminance and low-to-moderate saturation, limited to the head and hand region (above about 25% of the height or in the 0° front-facing area). Apply **no** gamma lift or desaturation to those texels. Instead apply a warm shift towards the target's 0° face skin colour; sample it from `targets/angle_000.png`, around (190,140,115). Also cut the cloth's `tex_sat` reduction to about 0.9, and add a light unsharp mask (radius 2 px, amount about 0.5) to the cloth to recover crisp plaid edges. Judge visually against `p000`: the skin should read as tan, not grey. Ignore colour diff, which is already fitted.
3. **Spend the single generation on the 4-view ablation, and keep the post pipeline identical.** Run `config_trellis_4view_quota_failed.json` (views 0/90/180/270, same seed), with its `post` block extended to the full iter_3 settings plus recs 1 and 2. Compare against the iter_3 mesh with the same post-processing, looking specifically at 90° and 135° IoU and the per-row torso depth table above. If 4 views do not make the profile at least 10% deeper, keep the iter_2 raw mesh for iteration 5 and use that last iteration only for tuning the post-processing (scale sweep and skin), not for another generation. Separately, for the orchestrator, outside the designer's scope: tighten `render_mask` or despill the green edge (for example, treat pixels where G > max(R,B) + 60 as background), so the green fringe stops costing IoU at every angle.
