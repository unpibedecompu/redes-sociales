# The LessWrong/Alignment Forum Continual Learning Sequence

*By Rauno Arike, RohanS, Owen Terry, Achu Menon, Zhijing Jin, Francis Rhys Ward, and Seth Herd. Posted June 12–24, 2026. Pulled from the continual-learning briefing compiled earlier.*

A quick correction first: I earlier described this as a "7-post sequence" — that was my error. The briefing file only documents **5 posts** by this author team, in this order:

- **["Implications of Continual Learning for LLM Agents: Introduction"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qChDifwpY8znER7cW/implications-of-continual-learning-for-llm-agents)** — June 12, 2026. Framing post introducing the series' two major failure pathways: post-deployment value/goal change, and loss of the "last-mover advantage" that pre-deployment audits and control protocols depend on.
- **["What's Continual Learning, and Why Might We Expect to See It in Advanced LLM Agents?"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5mCJzimtNZc9o4e26/what-s-continual-learning-and-why-might-we-expect-to-see-it)** — June 12, 2026. Definitional groundwork; argues deployment-time learning is "the most central safety-relevant feature" of continual learning.
- **["How Might Continual Learning Affect Safety and Alignment?"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j2zBqt7AksoEoHXNp/how-might-continual-learning-affect-safety-and-alignment)** — June 13, 2026. Identifies the two core risk pathways in depth: goals/values changing post-deployment (loss of data-source control, "value systematization," memetic spread of values across agent instances via shared memory), and loss of the last-mover advantage.
- **["Angles of Attack for Continual Learning Safety"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FKggLpnfbpbYvnjfG/angles-of-attack-for-continual-learning-safety)** — June 16, 2026. Proposes mitigations: prefer auditable, text-based memory over opaque weight-based learning, and keep humans in the rollback/control loop for deployment-time updates.
- **["Perspectives on Continual Learning: Survey Results and Forecasts"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qZrbhoaEALFTmyidr/perspectives-on-continual-learning-survey-results-and)** — June 24, 2026. Surveys AI safety researchers: universal agreement that continual learning expands the adversarial fine-tuning/poisoning attack surface, but disagreement on the timeline for significant goal drift, and skepticism that safety-focused CL research can keep pace with capabilities work.

When I checked the sequence's own intro page directly, it also referenced a planned 6th entry — a literature review on continual learning approaches, benchmarks, and neuroscience perspectives — but I wasn't able to confirm a published link for it, so it's left off this list rather than guessed at.

- **["AI 20240"](https://ai-2040.com/?choices=plan-a-root#example-detailed-regulatory-process)** — How does AI regulation works in Plan A:

For example, in 2031 a Chinese company gets some interesting preliminary results in continual learning. They think that if they invest more in that direction, they might be able to make an AI architecture that learns on the job from relatively small amounts of data. Thanks to the total research transparency, this breakthrough is quickly noticed by companies and nonprofits all over the world. A frantic conversation begins. On the one hand, continual learning would unlock huge economic value. On the other hand, safety cases currently depend on studying the safety properties of a model before it is deployed. If models could pick up new capabilities during deployment, that would invalidate the whole approach. And insofar as there are covert AI projects out there, it would be a huge gift to them. This conversation happens in public, rather than behind closed doors. A bunch of people get increasingly worried; the relevant regulators in China think it’s fine but the relevant regulators and third party risk assessors in the US are convinced that this is pretty scary and should be banned. It escalates to the President. He calls Xi Jinping. They bargain and threaten. They yell at each other. Ultimately Xi agrees to ban this type of thing if the US does too. Details are left to the respective regulators to hash out.93
