- "The Nixon Administration's Decision to End U.S. Biological Warfare Programs"

# Summary

- Meselson, Kissinger's former Harvard colleague, "provided Kissinger with studies demonstrating the high risk and limited utility of biological weapons."
- May 28, 1969: Kissinger orders studies of the US chemical and biological weapons programs (NSSM 59).
- November 10, 1969: report answering the study.
- November 18, 1969: National Security Council meeting.
- November 25, 1969: Nixon announces at Fort Detrick that the US renounces biological weapons (NSDM 35).
- February 20, 1970: Nixon also renounces toxins, whether made biologically or by chemical synthesis (NSDM 44).
- Neither Nixon nor Kissinger mention this decision in their memoirs.

### source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve02/d165

### author: Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State
- National Security Decision Memorandum 35
- Signed by Henry A. Kissinger
- November 25, 1969 (Top Secret)

# Summary

- "The United States will renounce the use of lethal methods of bacteriological/biological warfare."
- Also renounces all other methods (for example, incapacitating agents).
- Programs limited to research for defensive purposes.
- The Secretary of Defense recommends how to dispose of existing stocks.
- The US supports the British draft convention (presented August 26, 1969).
- The CIA keeps watching other countries' bioweapons capabilities.
- The 1925 Geneva Protocol is sent to the Senate.
