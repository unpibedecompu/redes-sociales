# Summary

- Meselson: Harvard molecular biologist, known for the Meselson-Stahl experiment (how DNA copies itself) and for advising the US government during the Cold War.

## How he learned about the US anthrax program (1963)

- Paul Doty (Harvard chemist) nominated him to the Arms Control and Disarmament Agency (ACDA), created under JFK.
- Summer 1963: one of six academic consultants.
- His first assignment was arms control of nuclear weapons in Europe, which he knew nothing about.
- He went to the CIA: they had "suspicions, but very little hard knowledge" about other countries' bioweapons.
- Then he visited Fort Detrick (Maryland). A colonel showed him a seven-story building and told him:
    - "We make anthrax spores. We have a 10,000-gallon reactor."
    - Why? Because it would be cheaper than nuclear weapons as a strategic weapon.
- The US weaponized anthrax and tularemia.

## His argument

- Bioweapons are a super cheap strategic weapon -> they help everyone else catch up to the US.
- "What if you could make hydrogen bombs for a dollar a piece?"
- "It's our interest to keep strategic weapons so expensive that nobody could afford them but us."
- Goal: "don't have it in the first place", not deterrence.
- Strategy: private papers, no public articles. "Go to the top" -> the person who could stop it: the President.

## Lobby with Kissinger and Nixon

- 1963-64: ACDA director William Foster asked McGeorge Bundy's national security staff to look at the issue. Bundy answered "No, you do it" -> Meselson got backing to write papers.
- Meselson and Kissinger were friends at Harvard (lunches, Harvard/MIT arms control seminar from 1966).
- After Nixon named Kissinger National Security Advisor (1969), they met by chance at Boston's airport:
    - Kissinger: "What should I do about your thing?"
    - Meselson: he would write papers.
- According to Meselson ("Henry told me"), Kissinger gave Nixon the novel "The Andromeda Strain".
- 1969: Nixon ends the US offensive biological weapons program.
- 1970, toxins: Meselson wrote "What Policy for Toxins".
    - Joint Chiefs: toxins made by chemists should be allowed.
    - Kissinger called him late at night: "We found Meselson's paper. The president has made his decision: we will consider toxins to be prohibited no matter how they're made."
    - The section of the paper that mattered most to Nixon: "The Authority and Credibility of the President".

## BWC

- The British already had a draft bioweapons treaty, the US decided to support it.
- The verification protocol never happened -> the US pulled out of the negotiations.
- "it's good to have a treaty even if there's no verification"
