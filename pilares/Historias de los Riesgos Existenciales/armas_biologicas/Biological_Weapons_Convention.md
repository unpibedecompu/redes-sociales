- Biological Weapons Convention (BWC)

# Summary

## From the idea to the treaty

- 1925, Geneva Protocol: bans the use of chemical and biological weapons, but not having or developing them.
    - In practice it was a "no first use" agreement. The US and the USSR kept their programs.
- 1968: the UK proposes separating biological from chemical weapons and negotiating a bioweapons treaty first.
- 1969-1972: negotiations at the Conference of the Committee on Disarmament (Geneva).
- 1969: Nixon ends the US offensive program and supports the British proposal.
- March 1971: the USSR and its allies stop opposing it and present their own draft.
- August 5, 1971: the US and the USSR present identical drafts.
- April 10, 1972: opened for signature in London, Moscow and Washington.
- March 26, 1975: enters into force after 22 ratifications (including the US, UK and USSR, the depositaries).
- First treaty to ban a whole category of weapons.

## Main article

- Article I: never develop, produce, stockpile or acquire biological agents or toxins without a peaceful justification, or the weapons to use them.

## Weaknesses

- No verification: a verification protocol was negotiated in 1995-2001 and rejected by the US in July 2001.
- The USSR violated it: Biopreparat, up to 65,000 people in hundreds of facilities (smallpox, plague, anthrax...).
- Sverdlovsk 1979: an anthrax leak from a military facility killed approximately 65 to 100 people. The USSR blamed contaminated meat. Yeltsin later admitted "our military developments were the cause".

- List of parties to the Biological Weapons Convention

# Summary

- 189 states parties (as of May 2025). Most recent: Kiribati (May 2025).
- Signed but not ratified (4): Egypt, Haiti, Somalia, Syria.
- Never signed (4): Chad, Djibouti, Eritrea, Israel.
- Taiwan ratified before 1971 (as the Republic of China) but is not counted in the 189.
